    Research Interests

    My research lies at the intersection of computer vision, video coding, 3D/4D scene modeling, and embodied intelligence. I am particularly interested in building systems that can represent, reconstruct, compress, and generate dynamic visual worlds — and enabling intelligent agents to perceive and act within them — bridging the gap between human perception and machine intelligence.

    I am currently focusing on four closely connected directions:

    🌍 1. World Models

    A world model is a learned internal simulator of the physical and visual world: given past observations, it predicts how the scene will look, move, and respond to actions in the future. World models lie at the heart of next-generation visual intelligence — they unify perception, prediction, and decision-making within a single generative framework, and they are the foundation for embodied agents that need to imagine before they act.

    My interest is in building world models that are:

    • Visually grounded — trained directly on real-world video rather than purely synthetic data, so the model captures genuine scene physics, lighting, and motion;
    • Long-horizon and consistent — capable of rolling out coherent, drift-free predictions over many seconds rather than collapsing after a few frames;
    • Geometry-aware — leveraging explicit 3D/4D representations (Gaussians, neural fields) so that imagined futures remain spatially plausible;
    • Controllable — supporting action, camera, and semantic conditioning so the model becomes a usable simulator for downstream tasks (planning, content creation, embodied AI).

    I see world models as the unifying lens that ties together my other three directions: 4D reconstruction provides the spatio-temporal substrate, embodied intelligence provides the perception-action loop, and multi-view coding provides the efficient sensory pipeline that feeds them. Together, they form a closed loop of perceive–understand–predict–act, which I believe is the path toward truly intelligent systems in the physical world.

    🤖 2. Embodied Intelligence

    Embodied intelligence refers to agents that can autonomously perceive, reason, decide, and act in physical or simulated environments — they do not just see the world; they do things in it. Unlike purely visual understanding, embodied intelligence requires closing the loop between perception and action: observing the environment, understanding its structure and dynamics, planning a course of action, executing it, receiving feedback, and continuously adapting.

    My group’s deep expertise in 4D reconstruction and multi-view perception provides a natural foundation for entering this space: 4D scene representations are essentially a structured understanding of the physical world, and multi-view coding gives agents an efficient sensory pipeline for environmental perception. I am exploring how to transform these capabilities into usable spatial cognition and interaction primitives for embodied agents.

    I am especially interested in:

    • Spatial perception and scene understanding — building structured, actionable representations of dynamic environments from 4D observations;
    • Interactive scene reconstruction — agents that actively gather information as they explore, incrementally building more complete scene representations;
    • Perception-action loops — moving from passive observation to active exploration, where reconstruction serves action rather than existing as an end in itself.

    Representative work: Actively exploring this direction.

    🎞️ 3. 4D Reconstruction and Generation

    A 4D scene is a 3D scene that evolves over time — exactly the kind of signal humans actually see. The goal of 4D reconstruction is to recover a photorealistic, temporally coherent, freely-navigable representation of a dynamic event from limited inputs (monocular video, sparse multi-view, or even noisy streaming captures), and the goal of 4D generation is to synthesize such dynamic worlds from priors when observations are missing.

    This direction is technically demanding because dynamic scenes break almost every assumption that static 3D reconstruction relies on: the geometry changes every frame, observations are extremely sparse in time, and any reconstruction error compounds across long sequences. Practical deployment further requires the representation to be streamable, error-resilient, and fast to render, so the user can navigate the captured event in real time without waiting for the whole video to be processed.

    I am especially interested in:

    • Streamable 4D representations that can be progressively built and rendered as new frames arrive, instead of requiring offline batch reconstruction;
    • Error-resilient long-horizon reconstruction, where the model can recover from drift, occlusion, and missing observations over hundreds or thousands of frames;
    • Bridging reconstruction and generation, so that the same representation can be both fitted to real captures and imagined by a generative prior — a natural stepping stone toward video-based world models.

    Representative work:
    SoLAR: The First Error-Resilient Streamable Long-Horizon Free-Viewpoint Video Reconstruction with Anchor Activation and Latent Recalibration
    Haotian Zhang, Xu Mo, Yixin Yu, Guanhua Zhu, Jian Xue, Tongda Xu, Yan Wang, Jiaqi Zhang, Siwei Ma, and Wen Gao · reviewed by TPAMI.

    SoLAR is, to our knowledge, the first error-resilient streamable free-viewpoint video framework that maintains stable reconstruction quality over long sequences without requiring GOP partitioning. Anchor Activation Dynamics better captures complex non-rigid transformations for improved scene compactness and representational capacity. Latent Discrepancy Aware Recalibration, inspired by bit allocation theory, identifies discrepancies between latent representations and recalibrates correspondences encoded in the network, effectively mitigating error propagation while preserving real-time performance and storage efficiency.

    📡 4. Multi-View Image / Video Compression

    Multi-view capture (light-field arrays, multi-camera rigs, distributed cameras for VR/free-viewpoint video) generates an enormous amount of data, but the views are highly redundant: they observe the same physical scene from different angles, so a smart codec should be able to exploit inter-view geometric correlation to compress them at a fraction of the cost of independently coding each view.

    The classical answer to this problem is distributed source coding, but modern learned codecs have an even more attractive option: directly aligning the cross-view content inside the network so that one view can serve as side information for another. The challenge is that the alignment must respect parallax — the same object appears at different positions and scales across views — and traditional self-attention or optical-flow-based alignment is either too expensive or fundamentally view-asymmetric.

    My focus in this area is on:

    • Parallax-aware attention mechanisms that align multi-view content efficiently without requiring explicit depth or disparity estimation;
    • Distributed coding architectures in which a “main” view is coded conventionally while side views are coded with cross-view conditioning, achieving near-joint-coding performance with strictly distributed encoders;
    • Bridging compression with reconstruction and world modeling — a multi-view codec that preserves geometric structure is the natural sensory front-end for both 4D reconstruction and downstream world models.

    Representative work:
    Parallax to Align Them All: An OmniParallax Attention Mechanism for Distributed Multi-View Image Compression
    Haotian Zhang, Feiyue Long, Yixin Yu, Guanhua Zhu, Jian Xue, Tongda Xu, Yan Wang, Jiaqi Zhang, and Siwei Ma · accepted by CVPR 2026.

    We propose OmniParallax Attention (OPAM), a general mechanism for explicitly modeling correlations and aligned features between arbitrary pairs of information sources with cubic computational complexity. Building on OPAM, the Parallax Multi-Information Fusion Module adaptively fuses multi-source information for the joint decoder and entropy model. ParaHydra is the first DMIC method to significantly surpass SOTA MIC codecs while maintaining excellent scalability — performance gains become increasingly pronounced as the number of views scales up.
